# Features

Every change to this app, and every change we have considered. One row per
change, in both tables.

- **`specs/<N>-<title>/`** is where a change is designed and built. The folder
  stays after it ships — it is the record, not scratch.
- **Implemented** is the archive: what landed, when, and what it produced.
  `/implement` writes the row.
- **Candidates** is the backlog: things worth building, with enough of a note to
  pick one up cold. A candidate is not a commitment and carries no number until
  `/brainstorm` allocates one.

Numbers are never reused. `N` for a new change is one higher than the largest
in either table.

| [5](5-the-first-beat/) | The first beat | 2026-09-17 | The app makes a sound. `globalTime` and `baseBeat` are the first two stages of a pipeline whose stage list is deliberately open — every stage is `(bar, ctx) => bar`, `PIPELINE` is an ordered array, and adding one is a file plus a line ([ADR 0007](../docs/adr/0007-the-drummer-is-a-pipeline-of-same-signature-stages.md)). Under them: ADR 0001's grid parser, the ten grids of `docs/music.md` Part 2 as data, a sample player over V4's kit, and a 100 ms-lookahead scheduler on the audio clock. Play/Stop and Tempo are live; swing, Feel, the mutes and the auto- buttons stay dummies, and the app plays dead straight until the swing stage lands. The purity rule in `docs/coding-guidelines.md` decided the shape: the whole drummer is pure and sits in `src/lib/`, the `AudioContext` and the loop are a new `src/features/transport/` slice, and because no feature may import a feature, `src/app/page.tsx` composes the two and `tsc` is the only thing holding `PanelControls` and `TransportControls` in agreement ([ADR 0008](../docs/adr/0008-the-drummer-is-domain-the-transport-is-a-slice.md)). `usePanelState` is gone and `Panel` is fully controlled. The `musician` wrote the missing grid→articulation map into `docs/music.md` Part 3 and found that Four On The Floor opens the hat on every upbeat and writes nothing after it, so Part 3's "always closed on the next grid step" cannot be met by notation and the choke group is required rather than optional. Two criteria were proven by experiment rather than asserted: moving a note and changing a level in a real groove left all 334 `src/lib/` tests green, and appending a real third stage took `pipeline.test.ts` from 37 tests to 42 with no edit to the file. The verifier also caught `swing` carrying two units under one name — a percentage on a groove, a ratio in the time module — which was closed by making it a percentage everywhere. **D1 was settled by ear the same day** — Fred: "it sounds good" — with one change asked for on hearing it, and made before commit: tempo and Stop took a whole bar to land, because the scheduler committed a bar to the audio clock the moment its bar line entered the 100 ms window. Pressing Stop left 2.5 s of drumming at 96 BPM. It now commits **one beat at a time**, so both land inside a beat; Stop lets the committed beat finish and lets an open hat ring out rather than cutting it. The pipeline still computes a bar — velocity curves and fills are bar-shaped — but `BarContext` anchors on an absolute beat, so a tempo change re-anchors mid-bar and no stage sees a difference. `docs/music.md`'s binding "takes effect at the next bar line" and ADR 0007 were both amended. 1022 tests. |

| [6](6-velocity-curves-and-humanize/) | Velocity curves and humanize | 🚧 | Two more pipeline stages, so `PIPELINE` is now `[globalTime, baseBeat, velocity, humanize]`. `velocity` multiplies the level value by a per-bar sine arc (0.92–1.05, floor on the One, peak at step 8) and a four-value phrase table (0.90/0.98/1.04/1.08); `humanize` writes `offsetBeats` as a split draw — one offset shared by every voice on a step, a smaller independent one per voice — clamped and zero-biased. Produced [ADR 0009](../docs/adr/0009-every-random-decision-is-a-hash-of-its-coordinates.md), [ADR 0010](../docs/adr/0010-the-lookahead-covers-what-the-pipeline-can-do-to-a-note.md) and [ADR 0011](../docs/adr/0011-a-note-carries-its-level-and-velocity-stops-identifying-it.md). **Three things were found by building rather than by reading.** The tech spec froze an exemption predicate — "backbeats and beat-1 kicks" — that the library breaks in one file: Four On The Floor Funk anchors the kick on all four quarters, so `1.00 × 1.05 × 1.08 = 1.134` would have pushed a velocity past 1.0 against the never-change list, and made the four kicks of a four-on-the-floor uneven. The exemption is the level now, which is ADR 0011. The scheduler plans each beat one clamp-width early, because `humanize` can pull a note back across its beat line and `start(t)` with a past `t` fires immediately — the margin at 60 BPM had collapsed from 75 ms to 35 ms. And the seeded hash moved to `src/lib/random/hash.ts` with a Box–Muller draw on top, because the scheduler computes each bar once per beat and a stream would give the four beats of a bar four different performances of it. ADR 0007 held on its first real test: two stages appended took `pipeline.test.ts` from 42 tests to 48 with no edit to the file — the two edits it did need were hand-built `Note` literals missing the new `level`, which `npm test` could not see and only `tsc` caught. **Then Fred heard it and humanize was cut by a factor of three before the change was done.** It first shipped `docs/music.md`'s σ = 0.026 beats with a ±0.065 clamp; his verdict was "humanize is too much, it should only be micro-timing, barely noticeable, yet much more human like". The document's own evidence agreed — at 96 BPM that was σ ≈ 16 ms against the 16–30 ms window it records as where a listener hears displacement as *deliberate*, so 32.5% of notes sat at or past the line. The `musician` found the root error rather than just turning the knob down: σ = 0.026 was measured across an expert rhythm **section**, which pools several players and so already contains the between-instrument offsets this app sets to zero, and applying it per note double-counted them. It is now derived top-down — clamp ±0.024 just inside the perceptual threshold, sigmas at clamp / 3, giving σ = 0.0081 combined. Measured after the change: σ = 5.04 ms at 96 BPM, 0.30% of draws on the clamp, and **0.00% of notes past 16 ms**. The margin the scheduler protects did not move, because the horizon and the displacement it covers are the same quantity — which is the whole argument of ADR 0010, confirmed by the clamp falling by a factor of 2.7 underneath it while every margin test stayed green. One scheduler test did need an edit — it compared the 60 and 180 BPM horizons with a hardcoded `+ 0.04` gap, now derived. Produced [ADR 0012](../docs/adr/0012-humanize-models-one-drummer-not-an-ensemble.md). |
| [7](7-respect-the-swing-knob/) | Respect the swing knob | 🚧 | The SWING knob had moved a number nothing read since V3, and V5 left the seam open on purpose — `stepBeats(step, swingPercent)` took the percentage and discarded it. V7 fills it in: `src/lib/time/swing.ts` warps the sixteenth grid so the second and fourth sixteenth of each beat are delayed and the beats and "&"s stay put, `stepBeats` delegates to it, and the scheduler holds a live percentage that `start` takes and `setSwing` replaces. **Swing turned out not to be a pipeline stage**, against what [ADR 0007](../docs/adr/0007-the-drummer-is-a-pipeline-of-same-signature-stages.md) and V5's own spec both recorded — it answers "when is step 3" rather than "what is in this bar", so it sits below the pipeline and `PIPELINE` did not change. That is [ADR 0013](../docs/adr/0013-swing-warps-the-grid-below-the-pipeline.md), and it is what makes *swing warps the grid rather than offsetting individual voices* arithmetic instead of a rule each stage has to remember; `offsetBeats` stays humanize's alone, so a measured displacement can still be attributed to the stage that made it. A swing change lands at the next beat line like a tempo change, but unlike `setTempo` it does not re-anchor the performance — swing never moves a beat line, only steps 1 and 3 inside a beat, which is what made "next beat" free rather than a new rule. **Three things came out of building it.** The frozen contract forces a cycle between `grid.ts` and `swing.ts`, and it survives only because `swungStepBeats` reads `STEPS_PER_BEAT` inside its body. Hoisting it to a module-scope const breaks every swung time **silently**: the import reads as `undefined` through a namespace object the cycle has not finished populating, so the pair size becomes `NaN` and nothing throws. The verifier caught the first draft of this record claiming a TDZ `ReferenceError` — it is 15 `expected NaN to be 0.25` assertions across both test files instead, which is a worse failure to diagnose and the reason it is written down. No lint rule sees it, so a named test holds it. Track B reached for the repo's first `vi.mock` of an internal path to prove the hook forwards to the scheduler, which `docs/testing.md` rules out; it came back out once Track A had landed, replaced by two seeded, times-based tests that read the real scheduled times off the fake `AudioContext` and compare a straight performance against a swung one at the same seed — they also pin "the next beat and not the committed one", and both mutants die by name. And the two existing tempo tests had to hold the swing straight: at the resting 54% a 0.02-beat warp plus a humanize draw of up to 0.024 sits right against the test's own `2 × CLAMP_BEATS` grid slack, so one run in three went red on a random seed. That is the spec's own risk — **the app swings from the moment this lands**, because the knob rests at 54% and the knob wins — showing up as a flake before it showed up by ear. The top of the knob is 0.10 ms shy of a true triplet at 96 BPM and left alone. Amended ADR 0007 and `docs/music.md` twice. 1252 tests. **D4 is open**: the 54%-at-96-BPM ear check is Fred's and has not been made. |
